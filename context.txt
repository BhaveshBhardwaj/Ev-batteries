EV Battery Health Certification Engine State-of-Health (SoH) Valuator for India's 2W/3W Fleet
Introduction and Ecosystem Context
The transition to electric mobility within the Indian subcontinent represents one of the most significant energy and transportation paradigm shifts of the twenty-first century. Facilitated by aggressive government mandates, production-linked incentives (PLI), and an expanding domestic manufacturing ecosystem, the electric vehicle (EV) sector is undergoing unprecedented acceleration. Unlike Western markets, which are structurally dominated by high-end, long-range passenger four-wheelers (4W), the Indian electrification trajectory is overwhelmingly driven by the two-wheeler (2W) and three-wheeler (3W) segments. These specific categories currently constitute the vast majority of electric vehicle sales and are projected to remain the vanguard of adoption, forming the backbone of urban micro-mobility, last-mile logistics, and commercial ride-hailing services.   

However, the long-term sustainability and financial viability of this rapid adoption curve are currently jeopardized by a fundamental market failure surrounding the vehicle's most critical and expensive component: the lithium-ion battery pack. Batteries represent approximately forty percent of the upfront capital expenditure of an electric vehicle. Yet, the secondary market for electric vehicles is paralyzed by a severe lack of transparency regarding battery degradation. In the traditional internal combustion engine (ICE) automotive ecosystem, asset valuation relies on established proxies such as odometer readings, vehicle age, and mechanical inspections. In the realm of electric mobility, these metrics are notoriously unreliable indicators of actual battery health. Two identical electric vehicles with identical mileage can possess drastically different battery capacities depending on their historical thermal exposure, depth of discharge profiles, and charging architectures.   

This profound information asymmetry has catalyzed a cascading series of bottlenecks across the broader automotive and financial sectors. Financial institutions, particularly Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs), are unable to accurately underwrite residual values for second-hand electric vehicles, leading to exorbitant risk premiums, punitive interest rates, or outright loan rejections. Concurrently, automotive insurance providers struggle to calculate accurate, risk-adjusted premiums or process accident claims due to the absence of verifiable pre-incident battery health data. Fleet operators are left incapable of forecasting asset depreciation or optimizing predictive maintenance schedules, thereby elevating the risk of catastrophic failures such as thermal runaway.   

To resolve this structural impediment, a sophisticated, highly localized diagnostic infrastructure is required. The present report outlines the comprehensive research, technical architecture, and commercialization roadmap for the "EV Battery Health Certification Engine State-of-Health (SoH) Valuator," a project proposed for incubation at Delhi Technological University (DTU) under the stewardship of Prof. Girish Kumar. Operating within the Automotive Technology theme, this innovation introduces an API-first, Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platform designed specifically to interpret the unique operational profiles and extreme thermal stresses characteristic of India’s 2W/3W fleets. By circumventing the need for proprietary hardware and leveraging advanced machine learning paradigms to extract electrochemical insights directly from raw Battery Management System (BMS) telemetry, this engine seeks to establish the definitive valuation standard for the secondary electric vehicle economy.

The Genesis of the Problem: Thermal Stress and Market Paralysis
The operational realities of the Indian electric vehicle ecosystem diverge sharply from the conditions for which standard global battery diagnostic models were calibrated. To comprehend the necessity for a localized State-of-Health (SoH) valuation engine, it is essential to analyze the specific degradation vectors impacting commercial fleets in the subcontinent.

The dominant battery chemistries deployed in India, primarily Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP) and Nickel Manganese Cobalt (NMC), degrade through complex electrochemical pathways that are highly sensitive to environmental and operational variables. In temperate climates, liquid-cooled battery architectures maintain optimal operating temperatures. Conversely, the vast majority of Indian 2W/3W electric vehicles rely on passive air cooling. During the summer months, ambient temperatures frequently exceed 45°C. When coupled with the internal heat generated during charging and discharging, core cell temperatures can routinely spike to levels that trigger accelerated electrochemical degradation.   

This thermal stress primarily accelerates the loss of lithium inventory (LLI) due to the continuous, uncontrolled thickening of the Solid Electrolyte Interphase (SEI) layer on the anode. Furthermore, elevated temperatures lead to the evaporation or decomposition of the liquid electrolyte, resulting in severe conductivity loss and a concomitant rise in internal resistance (DCIR).   

Simultaneously, the duty cycles of Indian commercial fleets exacerbate mechanical degradation. Last-mile delivery and ride-hailing operators rely on high-frequency, stop-and-go driving patterns characterized by rapid torque demands. Rather than completing controlled, deep charge-discharge cycles, these fleets rely on opportunistic partial charging, frequently plugging in vehicles for short durations at varying states of charge. This operational behavior induces significant mechanical stress on the cathode lattice, leading to the loss of active material (LAM) through particle cracking and isolation.

The convergence of these localized degradation vectors renders global diagnostic models, which are trained on Western four-wheeler highway driving cycles, mathematically invalid for the Indian context. As the initial wave of electric vehicles deployed between 2020 and 2022 reaches the end of its primary warranty period, an estimated 128 GWh of battery capacity will enter the secondary market or retirement phases annually by 2030. This volume creates a massive secondary financing, resale, and circularity market estimated at ₹8,300 crore. Unlocking this capital requires a diagnostic tool capable of accurately parsing the chaotic telemetry generated by thermally stressed, passively cooled battery packs.   

Regulatory Imperative: The 2026 Battery Pack Aadhaar (BPAN) Framework
The commercial trajectory of the proposed SoH Valuator Engine is not solely driven by market demand; it is deeply intertwined with incoming national regulatory mandates. In an effort to enforce supply chain traceability, mitigate safety risks, and mandate a circular economy for critical minerals, the Government of India is slated to implement the Battery Pack Aadhaar Number (BPAN) system in 2026. Spearheaded by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH), the Department of Science & Technology (DST), and NITI Aayog, this framework represents a sweeping digitization of the battery lifecycle.   

The BPAN architecture is designed as a centralized digital spine that assigns a mandatory, unique 21-character alphanumeric identity to every electric vehicle battery (L, M, and N categories) and industrial energy storage system exceeding 2kWh in capacity. This identity functions analogously to a digital passport, linking the physical asset to a complex, multi-tiered database mapping its entire lifecycle from raw material extraction to secondary deployment and eventual recycling.   

The regulatory framework organizes data capture across three distinct tiers, presenting a significant compliance hurdle for automotive manufacturers and fleet operators. The first tier encompasses static offline data, encoded physically on the battery casing via the 21-character alphanumeric string. This string incorporates the Battery Manufacturer Identifier (BMI), the Battery Descriptor Section (BDS), and the unique Battery Identifier (BI) serial numbers. The second tier utilizes a mandatory QR code affixed to the pack, granting authorized field personnel offline access to static material compositions, initial baseline capacity, cell chemistry specifications, and carbon footprint parameters.   

The third tier, known as Battery Dynamic Data (BDD), represents the most technically complex aspect of the mandate. The regulatory guidelines require Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs), importers, and large fleet managers to continuously update a centralized, server-based portal with real-time operational metrics. This dynamic tier mandates the reporting of the battery's actual State of Health (SoH), cycle counts, thermal anomaly events, and safety status throughout its active lifecycle.

The BPAN Tier 3 dynamic data mandate exposes a massive capability gap within the Indian automotive ecosystem. While OEMs excel at mechanical assembly and vehicle distribution, the majority lack the advanced software engineering and machine learning capabilities required to extract precise, objective SoH metrics from chaotic field telemetry. Current Battery Management Systems report raw voltage and current, but translating that raw data into an auditable health percentage requires dedicated data science infrastructure.   

The proposed API-first SoH Certification Engine is strategically architected to serve as this automated compliance bridge. By integrating the engine's Application Programming Interface (API), commercial entities can seamlessly route their raw BMS data into the cloud processing environment. The engine algorithmically computes the required degradation metrics and subsequently formats and pushes the dynamic data updates directly to the official government BPAN registry. This alignment transforms the valuation engine from a discretionary operational tool into an indispensable, government-mandated regulatory compliance layer, virtually guaranteeing B2B adoption.   

Scientific Framework: Algorithmic Architecture and Telemetry Processing
Achieving laboratory-grade state-of-health estimation without relying on external testing hardware necessitates a radical departure from traditional diagnostic methodologies. The scientific foundation of the proposed engine relies on the advanced algorithmic processing of existing, highly noisy on-board sensor data to isolate distinct electrochemical aging signatures.   

The Limitations of Traditional Diagnostics
Historically, battery capacity estimation has relied heavily on Coulomb counting, a technique that integrates the measured current over time to determine the total charge flowing into or out of the cells. While conceptually simple, Coulomb counting is inherently flawed for long-term field diagnostics. The current sensors embedded in low-cost 2W/3W Battery Management Systems suffer from offset errors and continuous drift. Over thousands of partial charge cycles, these micro-errors accumulate, leading to massive integration drift that renders the capacity estimate useless. Furthermore, alternative methods such as Open Circuit Voltage (OCV) lookup tables require the vehicle to rest completely for extended periods to reach electrochemical equilibrium, a logistical impossibility for high-utilization commercial delivery fleets that operate near continuously.   

Incremental Capacity Analysis (ICA) and Differential Voltage Analysis (DVA)
To bypass the limitations of Coulomb counting and OCV tracking, the proposed engine employs Incremental Capacity Analysis (ICA) and Differential Voltage Analysis (DVA). These techniques shift the analytical focus from macro-level charge tracking to the micro-level electrochemical phase transitions occurring within the battery electrodes.

By utilizing machine learning algorithms to track the morphology of these specific peaks over time, the engine can precisely quantify the total capacity fade and identify the specific internal degradation mode, completely independent of the accumulated sensor drift that plagues Coulomb counting.

The proposed architecture resolves this challenge through a sophisticated, multi-stage machine learning pipeline. Initially, the raw voltage, current, and temperature time-series logs are ingested via the cloud API. An adaptive Kalman filter, working in conjunction with advanced moving average algorithms, is applied to aggressively smooth the raw telemetry, eliminating high-frequency sensor noise without distorting the underlying voltage curve.   

Following noise reduction, the algorithm addresses the challenge of erratic charging behaviors. Because commercial fleets rarely execute complete 0-100% charge cycles, the system must operate on fragmented data. The engine utilizes pattern recognition algorithms to identify stable, partial-charging windows, typically during the constant-current (CC) charging phase. Subsequently, Gaussian Process Regression (GPR)—a powerful, probabilistic machine learning technique—is deployed to reconstruct the missing segments of the voltage curve and accurately calculate the derivatives even from highly fragmented data arrays.   

The extracted peak features—specifically peak intensity, integrated area, and voltage location—are then fed into a localized predictive model. This model, utilizing architectures such as Random Forests or deep neural networks, is explicitly trained on proprietary datasets capturing the extreme thermal profiles and duty cycles of the Indian environment. The final output is an objective, certified State of Health percentage, an estimated remaining useful life (RUL), and a dynamic internal resistance risk score, all derived without ever physically touching the battery.   

Competitive Intelligence and Market Positioning
The global imperative for electric vehicle battery lifecycle management has spawned a rapidly expanding diagnostics market, populated by well-funded international incumbents and agile domestic startups. However, a granular analysis of the competitive landscape reveals a profound structural gap for a software-only, API-centric solution specifically tuned for the unique dynamics of the Indian commercial ecosystem.   

Global Market Incumbents
The international landscape is dominated by sophisticated software analytics firms such as Twaice, Recurrent, and Altelium. These entities offer highly advanced cloud-based digital twin models and predictive analytics. However, their core algorithmic architecture presents a fundamental misalignment with the Indian market. These models are predominantly trained on data derived from passenger four-wheelers operating in temperate European and North American climates, utilizing highly regulated, liquid-cooled battery architectures. When applied to the passively cooled 2W/3W packs subjected to the 45°C ambient heat and erratic ride-sharing duty cycles of the subcontinent, these Western-calibrated algorithms demonstrate severe predictive inaccuracies.   

Other global players, such as Aviloo and the recently launched startup vsNEW, provide highly accurate, independent SoH reports. However, their business models are inherently constrained by hardware dependencies. Both companies require the deployment of proprietary OBD-II hardware dongles or specialized plug-in devices that must be physically shipped to the vehicle to execute active or passive diagnostic testing. This hardware-heavy approach severely limits rapid scalability across highly distributed, low-margin fleets in developing economies.   

Domestic Competitors
Within the Indian context, several companies have emerged to address battery diagnostics, most notably Battery OK Technologies and The Energy Company.

Battery OK has gained significant market traction with its "EV Doctor Air" and "EV Doctor Pro" devices. The company addresses the diagnostic gap by utilizing a physical hardware tool that acts as an intermediary, plugging in between the vehicle's charger and the battery pack. The device executes a rapid, active 15-minute diagnostic protocol based on Direct Current Internal Resistance (DCIR) measurements. While this hardware-centric approach is highly effective and practical for local repair garages and secondary market dealerships conducting physical vehicle inspections, it inherently lacks infinite scalability. Procuring, deploying, and maintaining physical hardware across vast, distributed commercial fleets represents a significant capital expenditure constraint, and the process still mandates physical human intervention to connect the device to the battery.   

The Energy Company approaches the market through a different vector, offering a digital-twin model known as FlexiTwin. While sophisticated, the FlexiTwin platform is currently deeply integrated into a Battery-as-a-Service (BaaS) business model, functioning as a proprietary monitoring system for The Energy Company's own custom-manufactured battery packs and specific BMS architectures. It is not positioned as an agnostic, third-party software API capable of auditing legacy fleets or diverse OEM architectures.

Intellectual Property Strategy and the 2025 CRI Guidelines
Securing robust intellectual property protection for a software-based algorithm in India necessitates careful navigation of a highly complex and recently updated legal framework. Historically, Section 3(k) of the Indian Patents Act, 1970, expressly excluded mathematical methods, business methods, and "computer programmes per se" from patentability, creating significant hurdles for software innovators.   

However, the intellectual property landscape underwent a fundamental transformation with the issuance of the finalized Guidelines for Examination of Computer Related Inventions (CRIs) by the Indian Patent Office (IPO) on July 29, 2025. These pivotal 2025 guidelines consolidate years of High Court jurisprudence, providing a structured four-limb framework that explicitly clarifies the scope of Section 3(k). The guidelines emphasize that while abstract algorithms and business methods remain absolutely barred, a software invention can overcome the exclusion if it demonstrates a definitive, measurable "technical effect" or technical contribution that extends beyond the mere execution of code on a general-purpose computer.   

The guidelines explicitly recognize emerging technologies, including artificial intelligence and machine learning, as patentable subject matter provided they solve a specific technical problem and are described with sufficient technical specificity regarding model architecture and data processing interactions. Crucially, the 2025 framework clarifies that novel hardware is not a mandatory prerequisite for software patentability, provided the software dictates a tangible improvement in the functioning of a system.   

To secure comprehensive protection for the SoH Valuator, the project will execute a multi-tiered intellectual property strategy precisely aligned with the 2025 CRI parameters:

System-Level Patent Filing Demonstrating Technical Effect: The patent application will avoid claiming the Gaussian Process Regression algorithm or the Incremental Capacity Analysis mathematical formulas in isolation, as this would trigger a rejection under the "mathematical method" exclusion. Instead, the claims will be drafted to describe a comprehensive system and method for physically extending battery hardware lifespan and preventing thermal runaway. The claims will articulate how the continuous remote extraction of ICA features from telemetry triggers dynamically scheduled adjustments to the physical charge parameters managed by the BMS. By explicitly linking the software's analytical output to a tangible, physical improvement in battery hardware efficiency and safety, the invention clearly demonstrates the mandated "technical effect," safely circumventing the Section 3(k) restrictions.   

Software Copyright Registration: The specific logic flow, structural codebase, API endpoints, and client-facing dashboard user interfaces will be protected via software copyright registration, securing the exact expression of the programming.

Trade Secret Maintenance: The engine's most significant competitive moat is not the algorithmic structure itself, but the data that optimizes it. The localized datasets, containing thousands of hours of proprietary Indian 2W/3W thermal and degradation cycling data, and the resulting highly optimized machine learning model weights, will be strictly maintained as internal trade secrets, heavily protected by non-disclosure agreements and partitioned server access.   

Trademark Protection: The unique branding associated with the valuation engine and the visual certification marks provided to B2B clients (e.g., the final "Certified Health Score" badge) will be trademarked to build trust and brand equity within the secondary market.

12-Month Development and Implementation Roadmap

Phase 2 (Months 4-6): Algorithmic Refinement and Model Validation. With real-world data streaming into the cloud, the ML engineering team will aggressively train the predictive models. The team will benchmark the algorithmic outputs against physical laboratory tear-downs of select fleet batteries to ensure the target RMSE of <2.5% for capacity estimation holds true in the field.

Phase 3 (Months 7-9): Commercial API Development and Regulatory Alignment. Following algorithmic validation, the software engineering team will construct the commercial-grade API architecture and the B2B SaaS dashboard. Phase 4 (Months 10-12): Beta Deployment, IP Finalization, and Commercial Launch. Q: Prior art on the concept, if any (Max 1500 chars)
Prior art in battery health diagnostics relies heavily on physical laboratory equipment (e.g., Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy [EIS] or full charge-discharge cycle tests using bulky cyclers) which are impractical for field-grade diagnostics. Patent and academic prior art (e.g., US9891282B2, EP3531154A1) describe methods for estimating battery capacity by tracking voltage, current, and temperature, but typically require controlled, continuous, full charging profiles or specialized onboard sensors. Other methods extract dQ/dV (Incremental Capacity) features to estimate aging but are highly susceptible to sensor noise and BMS telemetry drift, particularly under irregular, partial charging profiles. The proposed solution improves upon this prior art by applying advanced filtering (e.g., Kalman filters combined with noise-tolerant Gaussian Process Regression) to isolate the core dQ/dV and dV/dQ peaks from partial, irregular charge logs generated by low-cost 2W/3W BMS sensors. It relies entirely on standard cloud computing to process these signals, removing any requirement for specialized hardware, active profiling, or laboratory testing.

Q: Main Problem Being Addressed in the Project (Max 1500 chars)
The core problem is the absolute lack of battery health transparency in the secondary EV market, which stalls the resale, financing, and insurance of used electric vehicles in India. Batteries represent 40% of an EV's upfront cost but degrade dynamically based on operating parameters, thermal cycles, and usage abuse. Standard metrics like odometer readings or vehicle age are highly inaccurate proxies for actual battery degradation. Currently, financial institutions hesitate to finance second-hand EVs because they cannot assess the residual value of the battery, resulting in exorbitant interest rates or flat rejections. Furthermore, existing global diagnostic tools fail in India because: 1) They require expensive, physical testing hardware that garages and fleet operators cannot afford or scale. 2) They are calibrated on Western four-wheelers driven on highways in moderate climates. India’s EV market is dominated by light 2W/3W fleets running frequent, short, high-stress trips under extreme ambient temperatures (exceeding 45°C) and heavy dust, which accelerate completely different aging mechanisms (such as rapid lithium plating and electrolyte dry-out). 3) Emerging regulations, specifically MoRTH's 2026 mandatory Battery Pack Aadhaar Number (BPAN) framework, require dynamic reporting of State of Health (SoH) and safety events. OEMs and fleet managers currently lack the lightweight software tools necessary to continuously report these dynamic parameters.
